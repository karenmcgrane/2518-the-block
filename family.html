The Stevens–Huff family

Four generations who between them owned or lived in 2518 South Street from 1930 to 1987 — and one of whom died in the front of it. Everything here comes from censuses, death certificates, a draft card, a marriage license and two newspaper death notices.

The line that matters

MICHAEL MALONEY, of Ireland → his daughter MARY, who came to Philadelphia about 1890 and married JAMES STEVENS → their daughter AGNES, who married ALEXANDER HUFF → their six children, born between 1916 and 1928, five of whom grew up in this house. Mary Stevens bought 2518 in 1924. Agnes Huff owned it from 1949 to 1984.

The tree

A dashed outline, or a dashed line, means the record does not establish the person or the connection.

Drawn from the 1920, 1930, 1940 and 1950 censuses; Pennsylvania death certificates 37810 (Mary Stevens, 1949) and 124465 (Alexander Huff, 1972); a 1942 draft registration; a 1944 marriage license; and Inquirer death notices of 1973, 1974 and 1984.

Two children who appear once and then do not. A Joseph aged 1½ is in the household in January 1920 and is not in the house in 1930 — and a second son was given the same name in 1928. An Alexander junior aged 5 is in the 1930 household, would have been fifteen in 1940, and is on neither the 1940 sheet nor his mother’s 1984 death notice, which names five children.

The likeliest reading is that both died in childhood, and re-using a dead child’s name was ordinary practice. This is an inference from two absences, not a finding. Philadelphia death records for a Joseph Huff, 1920–28, and an Alexander Huff, 1930–40, would settle both.
The one date that would fill the top of the tree. Mary’s naturalization, 1916. A naturalization file gives the exact birthplace in Ireland, the ship and date of arrival, and often the husband’s details — which would fix the town in Ireland she came from.
